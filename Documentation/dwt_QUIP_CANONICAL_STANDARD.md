# DWT Quip Canonical Standard

This document records the authoritative quip standard for the campaign.

## Core Model

Quips are short narrative verses inspired by the light, rhythmic storytelling mode of Tolkien's `Cat`.

They are not strict metrical poems. They are short spoken-feeling verses with:

- clear end rhyme
- flexible meter
- concrete imagery
- a conversational narrator voice

The target feel is something a sailor, dockhand, ferryman, bell-ringer, or wary villager might casually say inside the Ghosts of Velen setting.

## Length Buckets

### Short

- Lines: `2`
- Rhyme scheme: `AA`
- Use: one quick observation, joke, warning, or remark

### Medium

- Lines: `4`
- Rhyme scheme: `ACBC`
- Use: one compact scene, beat, or moment

### Long

- Lines: `8`
- Rhyme scheme: `ABCBDEFE`
- Use: one short story fragment, often ending in a twist, punchline, or quiet observation

## Voice and Style

### Rhythmic but Natural

- End rhyme should be audible and intentional.
- Meter can flex.
- Lines should still read naturally when spoken aloud.

### Conversational Narrative Tone

- Quips should feel spoken, not declaimed.
- The voice can be sly, dry, wistful, or lightly absurd.
- The voice should not sound academic, ornamental, or artificially epic.

### Limited Ornament

Use lightly:

- internal rhyme
- alliteration

Do not let sound-play overwhelm clarity.

## Setting Fit

Quips should favor concrete imagery grounded in Ghosts of Velen and adjacent maritime life.

Preferred image fields:

- weather
- docks
- harbor life
- bells
- saints
- lanterns
- wind
- tide
- ships
- rope
- gulls
- nets
- animals

Language should feel setting-appropriate for Forgotten Realms and Ghosts of Velen.

Avoid:

- modern idioms
- modern slang
- self-aware parody

## Acceptance Rules

A valid quip should satisfy all of the following:

- one coherent thought or moment
- correct line count for its bucket
- target rhyme scheme for its bucket
- spoken-feeling cadence
- concrete imagery
- setting-appropriate language
- structural variety

## Structural Variety Rule

Do not mass-produce quips from one reusable sentence shell.

Avoid:

- identical openings
- obvious noun-substitution templates
- repeated grammatical scaffolds across most of a pool

Each quip should feel authored, not mechanically permuted.

## Implementation Notes for This Branch

- The current corpus already respects the 2/4/8-line bucket counts.
- The next compliance pass should focus on rhyme quality, voice fit, maritime imagery, and structural variety.
- Region pools should be judged by this standard, not merely by whether they are grammatical.
- Strong older material can still be used as reference text, but it is not automatically canonical if it misses the Ghosts of Velen voice.
